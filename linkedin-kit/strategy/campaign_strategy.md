# Campaign Strategy — AI as Infrastructure

## The core idea
Most people show AI as a *feature*. I'm showing it as *infrastructure*.

The Career Intelligence Dashboard is the artifact. The real subject is a way of thinking:
**research → systems → AI workflows → product → scale.**

This campaign is not "I built an app." It's a public teardown of how a working AI product
actually gets designed, guarded, evaluated, and pointed at a business model.

## Positioning statement
> I transform complex systemic shifts into actionable strategy and tangible products.
> The Career Intelligence Dashboard is proof: labour-market data → analytics → a guarded AI
> layer → a usable dashboard → an evaluation loop → a productization path.

## Strategic objectives
1. **Reposition** the audience's view of me from "uses AI" to "builds systems with AI."
2. **Demonstrate** end-to-end product ability: data engineering, AI architecture, UX, evaluation, roadmap.
3. **Generate** inbound: recruiters (AI PM/strategy roles), founders (build partner), product leaders (peer credibility).
4. **Anchor** a reusable case study I can point to for years — a durable asset, not a disposable post.

## The wedge (why this works)
Everyone is posting AI content. Almost none of it is *grounded in a working system with real
guardrails and real evaluation numbers*. The differentiator is not the topic — it's the receipts:

- A live app anyone can open.
- A public repo anyone can read.
- Real evaluation metrics (80% text-to-SQL accuracy, 0 invented numbers, 0.94 faithfulness, 5/5 refusals).
- A design principle stated plainly: **the LLM never emits a number.**

That last line is the whole campaign in one sentence. Lead with it often.

## Message architecture
**North star:** AI strategy becomes real when it leaves the slide deck and becomes a working system.

**Three proof layers, repeated across the campaign:**
1. **Systems thinking** — data → analytics → AI → UX → evaluation → productization.
2. **Trust engineering** — grounding, validation, refusal. The system is the source of truth, not the model.
3. **Business sense** — an open dashboard is not a product yet; here's the path that makes it one.

## Content pillars (rotate — never post two of the same back to back)
1. Why I built it
2. The problem with generic career advice
3. System architecture
4. The data layer
5. The AI layer
6. Guardrails & evaluation
7. UX & dashboard design
8. Productization roadmap
9. Business-model possibilities
10. My personal operating system (how I use AI to stay ahead)

## Cadence
- **3 posts/week** for 4 weeks (12 core posts). Tue / Thu / Sat, ~8:30am ET.
- 1 carousel + 1 text post + 1 short reflection or "build log" per week.
- Reply to every substantive comment within 2 hours on posting day (algorithm + relationship).

## Formats & why
- **Carousels** — for architecture, guardrails, roadmap (spatial ideas need slides).
- **Text posts** — for the story, the problem, the reflections (voice carries these).
- **One-line + link** — occasional, to drive traffic to the live app / landing page.
- **Screenshots** — real dashboard frames (Ask, Advisor, Resume Studio) beat any illustration.

## Calls to action (soft, rotate)
- "The app is live — link in comments."
- "Repo's public if you want to see how the guardrails are wired."
- "I'll break down the next layer in the next post."
- "Curious what you'd add before this becomes a real product."

## Success signals (track, don't obsess)
- **Leading:** saves, meaningful comments, profile views, connection requests from PM/founders/AI builders.
- **Lagging (60 days):** ≥1 inbound conversation referencing the project; the Featured Section doing the framing on autopilot.

## Guardrails for the campaign itself (practice what the product preaches)
- No fake traction. No "users love it" without users.
- No "revolutionary / game-changing." Explain the mechanism instead.
- Never claim the product is scaled. It's a proof-of-work with a roadmap. Say exactly that.
- Every metric quoted is real and reproducible from the repo.
